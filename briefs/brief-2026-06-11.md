Today's research: India fintech's regulatory moat vs US SaaS consolidation
Domain: Fintech and Payments
────────────────────────────────────

TWEET OPTIONS

[A] The Number

₹1,000 crore: Cashfree's revenue with EBITDA profitability. They don't own payment rails (UPI does). They don't own customer data (banks do). Their moat? Vertical integration + credit measurement. India's regulator accidentally proved open systems can be more defensible than proprietary monopolies.
(279 chars)

[B] Counter-Narrative

Everyone says platform lock-in is the only durable moat. India's fintech proves otherwise: Cashfree, Razorpay, and Paytm can't lock in customers because UPI is mandated to be interoperable. So they compete on measurement and vertical depth instead. Result: faster profitability than US SaaS.
(278 chars)

[C] Historical Parallel

Today's India fintech looks exactly like 1990s telecoms after regulators forced number portability. Monopolies died. Winners were companies that went vertical (Verizon into content) not horizontal (AT&T tried to own infrastructure). Cashfree is betting measurement beats lock-in. History says yes.
(266 chars)

[D] The Quote

Cashfree CEO on hitting ₹1,000 crore: "UPI made 80% of payments interoperable, so we focused on verticals regulators can't commoditize: credit underwriting, merchant analytics." Translation: when you can't own infrastructure, own the measurement layer. That's the new moat in open systems.
