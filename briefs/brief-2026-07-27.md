Today's research: SaaS pricing collapse under agentic AI arbitrage
Domain: Enterprise AI / SaaS business models
────────────────────────────────────

TWEET OPTIONS

[A] The Number

$234B: That's how much SaaS spending Gartner says is exposed to agentic AI by 2030. The math is simple: agents execute work for $0.003/minute that companies pay $0.27/minute for with per-seat licenses. Per-seat pricing dies when the seat stops being full.
(267 chars)

[B] Counter-Narrative

Everyone thinks the AWS $1.7T billing glitch was just a bug. The real story: it revealed that SaaS pricing systems assume humans consume software. Agents break that. When one agent does the work of 5 employees, you should pay 5x less, not 5x the same.
(273 chars)

[C] The Number (pricing chaos)

$0.10 per action. That's where Salesforce landed after trying $2 per conversation. The chaos in pricing shows vendors know per-seat is dead but can't figure out what replaces it. Shift to consumption now and tank revenue, or wait 18 months and get hollowed out.
(274 chars)

[D] Historical Parallel

Today's SaaS pricing crisis looks exactly like the telecom collapse in 1999. AT&T priced on per-minute calls. VoIP made minutes free. The carriers that held old pricing lost 80% of market cap in 3 years. Salesforce and Workday face the same choice right now.
(270 chars)

[E] Counter-Narrative (market timing)

Gartner put a number on it: $234B in enterprise software spending at risk from agentic AI. Not someday. By 2030. The vendors see it: Salesforce shifted pricing twice in 6 months, Intercom went from per-seat to $0.99 per resolution. The model broke, they just can't say it.
