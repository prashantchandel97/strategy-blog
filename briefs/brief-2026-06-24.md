STRATEGY BRIEF | Wednesday, June 24

Today's research: Antitrust regulation threatens custom silicon moats
Domain: AI infrastructure and competitive strategy
────────────────────────────────────

TWEET OPTIONS

[A] The Number

€100M+: What Amazon, Google, Meta each spend annually on EU Digital Markets Act compliance. 47 dedicated FTEs at Amazon alone. Now AICOA (reintroduced June 10) would bring the same rules to the US, forcing hyperscalers to share custom chip cost advantages with competitors.
(276 chars)

[B] Counter-Narrative

Everyone expects Nvidia margins to compress from 78% to 62% by 2028 as hyperscalers deploy custom chips. But AICOA would force Amazon, Google, Microsoft to sell Trainium/TPU/Maia access to competitors at the same price. Nvidia's moat stabilizes. Regulation saves the incumbent.
(279 chars)

[C] Historical Parallel

Today's custom silicon moat looks exactly like Microsoft's browser bundling in 1998. DOJ forced unbundling, destroying the distribution advantage before it compounded. AICOA targets the same vertical integration playbook: Amazon's Trainium, Google's TPU must be shared, not captive.
(278 chars)

[D] The Number

2027-2028: When AICOA compliance would be required. Also 2027-2028: When Amazon's $20B Trainium business hits scale deployment. Hyperscalers get 18-36 months to build custom silicon moats before regulation forces them to share the 40-50% cost advantage with all cloud providers.
(279 chars)

[E] The Quote

AICOA bans self-preferencing: Amazon couldn't charge AWS less for Trainium than it charges Azure. Google couldn't give Google Cloud cheaper TPU access than competitors get. The custom chip advantage doesn't disappear, it just gets democratized. Moat becomes a commodity overnight.
