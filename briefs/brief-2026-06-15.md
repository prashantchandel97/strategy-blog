STRATEGY BRIEF | Monday, June 15

Today's research: India's fintech splits: measurement moats vs distribution wars
Domain: Fintech and Payments
────────────────────────────────────

TWEET OPTIONS

[A] Counter-Narrative

Kissht IPO'd at 12.7% net margins after deliberately refusing to lend more. This is the opposite of every fintech playbook: raise capital, burn it on growth, IPO on a narrative. India's capital markets just voted for unit economics over volume. Measurement moats beat scale moats.
(280 chars)

[B] The Number

₹199.3 crore: Kissht's net profit on ₹1,569.9 crore revenue (12.7% margin). Compare: Razorpay hit ₹3,783 crore revenue with 65% growth but still lost ₹1,209 crore. Indian capital markets are now pricing profitability over transaction volume. The re-rating is real.
(268 chars)

[C] Historical Parallel

India's fintech today looks exactly like 2015 US e-commerce: Amazon (distribution at scale, low margin) vs Wayfair (vertical depth, unit economics). Kissht and Aye Finance went vertical into lending with proprietary underwriting. Paytm and Google Pay stayed horizontal in payments. Capital markets picked the vertical players.
(280 chars)

[D] The Quote

Kissht CEO Anish Singh to investors: "We deliberately chose not to disburse more loans" to stay profitable. Translation: we built a measurement system good enough that we don't need venture-scale growth to justify our existence. That's the new fintech moat in India.
