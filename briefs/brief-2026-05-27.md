STRATEGY BRIEF | Wednesday, May 27

Today's research: The vertical integration trap for platform owners
Domain: AI Infrastructure / Antitrust
────────────────────────────────────

TWEET OPTIONS

[A] The Number

$64B: Arm's 2023 IPO valuation, based on licensing CPU architecture to Apple, Qualcomm, Nvidia. Now the FTC is investigating whether Arm can design its own chips without crushing licensees. If forced to divest the AGI CPU, it's a complete repricing of the business.
(275 chars)

[B] Counter-Narrative

Everyone assumes vertical integration makes companies stronger. But if you own a platform (CPU architecture, operating system, payment rails), moving downstream into products makes you a regulatory target. Arm and Microsoft both learned this in May 2026.
(270 chars)

[C] Historical Parallel

Arm's FTC probe mirrors Qualcomm 2009-2015: own a platform, license it to everyone, then compete downstream. Qualcomm paid $750M to settle antitrust charges. Arm's AGI CPU faces the same trap. Platform owners can't move into products without triggering regulatory scrutiny.
(276 chars)

[D] The Asymmetry

Nvidia is safe from antitrust because it's a pure-play chip designer. Apple is safe because it owns the full stack. But Arm (licensor now making chips) and Microsoft (OS owner bundling AI) face FTC/CMA probes. Platform owners who license to others can't move downstream.
(277 chars)

[E] The Bundling Trap

Microsoft bundles Copilot into Windows, Office, Teams, and Outlook. That's 500+ million Office seats with AI as the default. The UK CMA is investigating whether this locks out rivals like Google Duet and Anthropic. Same pattern as the 1990s IE antitrust case, now with AI.
(275 chars)
