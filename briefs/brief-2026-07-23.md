TWEET OPTIONS

[A] The Number

$125B: Meta's 2026 capex guidance, mostly for AI data centers. Now launching Meta Compute cloud business pricing GPUs 20 to 30% below AWS, Azure, and GCP. Not competing on operations. Competing on balance sheet size. If you can burn $50B yearly to subsidize infrastructure, you don't need to be better. You just need to be cheaper long enough to own distribution.

[B] The Quote

Mark Zuckerberg, July 1 earnings call: Meta is building a cloud infrastructure business to "monetize excess AI capacity." Translation: we front loaded so much GPU spend that we now have capacity overhang. Will sell compute at near cost to lock in AI startups before AWS can. Meta stock popped 9%. Investors believe distribution moat beats operational excellence.

[C] Counter-Narrative

Everyone says Meta is entering the cloud wars. Wrong framing. Meta is using a $1.49 trillion market cap to commoditize cloud infrastructure. AWS earns $22B profit annually from cloud (33% of operating profit). Meta doesn't need that margin. It just needs ecosystem lock in. If 50,000 startups train on Meta Compute plus Llama, switching costs emerge even if Meta never matches AWS SLAs.
(279 chars)

[D] Historical Parallel

Today's Meta Compute looks exactly like Amazon's 2006 AWS launch: subsidized infrastructure to absorb excess capacity, priced to undercut. Difference: Amazon needed cloud profits. Meta doesn't. It's playing for model ecosystem lock in (Muse Spark at 1/4 of OpenAI's rates). If this works, AWS owns enterprise. Meta owns the next 100,000 AI startups. Different games, different winners.
