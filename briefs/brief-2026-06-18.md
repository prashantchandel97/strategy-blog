Today's research: Zillow's $880M lesson on fake moats
Domain: Enterprise technology and capital allocation
────────────────────────────────────

TWEET OPTIONS

[A] The Number

$880M: Zillow's loss from iBuying (2017-2021). Zestimate had 1.8% error on market sales, 7% off-market. That 7% on a $500k home = $35k uncertainty. Fine for search. Fatal for buying homes with company capital. Moat validation at scale beats moat validation on historical data.
(279 chars)

[B] Historical Parallel

Zillow 2017-2021 looks exactly like today's SaaS AI bundling. Both moved early to consolidate before competitors. Both had data moats that looked real. Zillow: $880M loss when Zestimate broke at scale. Salesforce: 56% of customers report under 5% ROI. Same pattern, 5 years apart.
(279 chars)

[C] Counter-Narrative

Everyone says "consolidate early and win." Zillow did exactly that: vertically integrated home buying in 2017, proprietary algorithm, moved before Opendoor scaled. Lost $880M by 2021. The real rule: consolidate early only if you've validated the moat works at required precision.
(278 chars)

[D] The Quote

CEO Rich Barton: Zillow "didn't meaningfully test" whether Zestimate could predict prices for homes it owned with capital. Worked for 100M+ listings. Failed for iBuying. Moved fast to beat Opendoor, lost $880M in 18 months. Not all moats transfer to new use cases.
(275 chars)
