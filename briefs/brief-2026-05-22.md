TWEET OPTIONS

[A] The Number

5%:

That's Salesforce Agentforce adoption after 18 months and three different pricing models.

The problem isn't the product. It's that transparent AI costs kill adoption. Customers see "CRM + agent add-on" and flee.

Meanwhile Microsoft bundles Copilot into 365. Costs hidden. No new budget meeting needed.

[B] Counter-Narrative

Everyone thinks on-device AI will kill cloud inference demand.

But OpenAI's inference costs are growing 68% year over year. Not shrinking. Growing.

The market isn't collapsing. It's bifurcating: commodity queries move to device, frontier reasoning stays in cloud and gets more expensive.

(278 chars)

[C] Historical Parallel

Today's AI cost bifurcation looks exactly like the 1990s shift from mainframes to PCs.

Mainframes didn't die. They moved upmarket into banking and transactions (40% of enterprise compute today).

PCs took email and spreadsheets.

Same pattern now: on-device takes commodity queries, cloud keeps frontier reasoning.

(263 chars)

[D] The Quote

Salesforce changed Agentforce pricing three times in 18 months: $2 per conversation, then $0.10 per action, then per-seat hybrid.

What that signals to customers: "We don't know what this actually costs us, so we don't know what it should cost you."

Result: 5% adoption. Perpetual pricing whiplash kills trust.

(268 chars)

[E] The Number

$1.35:

OpenAI spends that much for every dollar it earns.

Per-token pricing is transparent (developers love it) but exposes that inference infrastructure is unsustainably expensive at current volumes.

Microsoft hides the same costs in 365 bundles. Customers never see them.

Transparency is a liability.
