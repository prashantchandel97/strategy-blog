[A] The Number

$460B: Google Cloud's backlog in Q1 2026, nearly double last year. Yet they told Meta 'no' on Gemini capacity. The limit isn't chips or demand. It's power infrastructure: AI racks need 30-110 kW vs. 5-15 kW traditional. Takes 2-4 quarters to build. That's why independent AI labs are disappearing.

[B] Historical Parallel

Today's AI capacity crunch looks exactly like 2000's bandwidth wars. Then: telcos controlled internet pipes, became kingmakers. Now: Google, Microsoft, Amazon control inference capacity, decide who can scale. Meta's $115B infrastructure response isn't spending, it's survival.

[C] Counter-Narrative

Everyone thinks model quality determines AI winners. Google told Meta 'no' on Gemini capacity in March. Meta's response: abandon open-source Llama, spend $115-135B on proprietary infrastructure. The real moat isn't model performance. It's guaranteed inference capacity.

[D] The Number (market structure)

3 tiers emerging in AI: Tier 1 mega-platforms ($100B+ capex, own infrastructure). Tier 2 model labs dependent on hyperscalers. Tier 3 API-only access. When Google told Meta 'no' on capacity, Meta moved from trying Tier 2 to forcing Tier 1. That $115B spend is the entry price.
