Today's research: SaaS pricing collapse under AI agents
Domain: Enterprise software & business model shifts
────────────────────────────────────

TWEET OPTIONS

[A] The Number

Atlassian's Q1 2026: revenue up, paid seats down. First time in 25 years a SaaS company grew revenue while losing customers. Why? AI automation means you need fewer people to get more done. Per-seat pricing just became structurally obsolete.

(260 chars)

[B] Counter-Narrative

Everyone thinks SaaS margins are safe at 75%. Reality: companies moving to usage-based pricing see 20-30% gross margin hit in first two years. Why? You're now paying for compute per API call, not just replicating digital licenses. Economics flip overnight.

(268 chars)

[C] The Quote

Zendesk now charges per automated resolution (72-hour confirm). You don't pay per support agent anymore, you pay per ticket their AI closes. This is happening: outcome-based pricing replacing headcount-based pricing across enterprise software.

(259 chars)

[D] Historical Parallel

SaaS pricing in 2026 looks exactly like electricity pricing in 1910. Started with flat fees per building, shifted to usage meters when industrial demand exploded. Now software has the same problem: AI agents consume unpredictably, flat pricing breaks.

(261 chars)

[E] Structural Change

Mid-market SaaS companies ($5-20B) are about to vanish. Usage-based pricing kills their 40% margins, but they're too small to bundle products like Salesforce. Goldman's May 2026 note flags it: expect $50B+ acquisitions, not IPOs, for the next 5 years.
