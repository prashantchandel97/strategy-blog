Today's research: Historical moat timelines (Intel, Microsoft, Cisco)
Domain: AI Infrastructure / Enterprise SaaS
────────────────────────────────────

TWEET OPTIONS

[A] The Number

25 years: Intel's vertical integration moat lasted a generation, then destroyed the company when foundry economics shifted. 15 years: Microsoft's bundling moat, abandoned just in time. Salesforce is forcing 170% price increases on AI bundling where 56% of customers see under 5% ROI. History says you get 3-20 years. Then the moat becomes an anchor.

(279 chars)

[B] Historical Parallel

Today's SaaS AI bundling looks exactly like 1999's Cisco buying Cerent for $6.9B. Promising tech, unvalidated at scale, paid peak price. Within 5 years, optical networking commoditized. Cisco never recovered the investment. Salesforce customers report under 5% ROI on Agentforce. Same playbook, same risk.

(278 chars)

[C] Counter-Narrative

Everyone thinks consolidating early means winning. Intel consolidated early (vertical integration), dominated for 25 years, then lost everything when TSMC distributed capex across customers. Microsoft consolidated early (bundling), saw the cliff coming, pivoted to cloud. The difference: willingness to abandon the moat before it kills you.

(277 chars)

[D] The Quote

"We deliberately chose not to disburse more loans" (Kissht CEO, on staying profitable). Sounds disciplined. But Kissht's 2.12% GNPA is industry average, not exceptional. When RBI opens lending to tech giants in 18 months, that measurement moat evaporates. Intel thought its fabs were defensible too.

(268 chars)

[E] The Number

$880M: What Zillow lost when Zestimate's 2% error on past sales became 7% error predicting future prices. $6.9B: What Cisco paid for Cerent's unvalidated optical tech. $920M/month: What Google pays SpaceX to hedge against its inference moat failing. Consolidation works until the measurement breaks.
