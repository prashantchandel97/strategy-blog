Today's research: The Great SaaS Bifurcation
Domain: Enterprise software economics and market structure
────────────────────────────────────

TWEET OPTIONS

[A] The Number

112 to 106: The average Fortune 500 cut 6 SaaS tools in 2024, yet spent 20% more. Why? They killed horizontal platforms (Slack, Asana) in favor of bundled suites (Microsoft, Salesforce) plus custom vertical tools. AI-assisted coding made custom software viable again: 3-4 months vs 12.
(273 chars)

[B] Counter-Narrative

SaaS consolidation looks like the death of subscriptions. It's not. Enterprises are killing generic tools (Slack, Jira) to pay MORE for fewer platforms (Microsoft, Salesforce) and custom builds. Losers: horizontal SaaS. Winners: bundlers and vertical specialists.
(263 chars)

[C] The Number

$5M in waste: That's what the average $20M SaaS buyer loses to unused entitlements. But you can't fix it by renegotiating. You fix it by cutting the tool entirely. Result: Fortune 500 companies went from 112 SaaS apps to 106, while spending climbed 20%. Fewer tools, higher bills.
(280 chars)

[D] Historical Parallel

Custom software was supposed to die in the 2010s, killed by SaaS economics. Now it's back. AI coding tools cut development from 12 months to 3-4 months. If your SaaS bill hits $10K/month, a $150K custom build pays for itself in 15 months. Enterprises are taking that trade now.
(278 chars)

[E] The Number

$3.2B: That's how much SaaS spend Spendflo's customers cut by 30-40% through better contract negotiation. But even that hit a ceiling. So they built Flo, an AI agent that automates the entire procurement cycle. You're no longer buying a SaaS tool. You're buying an outcome.
