## Core Thesis
The era when software companies could capture margin has ended. AI agents shattered SaaS's 25-year per-seat unit economics, but the real structural shift is deeper: margin is migrating from application layers (SaaS, fintech) to infrastructure layers (cloud, compute, chips). Meta, Google, Microsoft, and Amazon are now competing on infrastructure control, not model capability. Companies like Salesforce and HubSpot are surviving by embedding agents into existing customer lock-in *and* moving fast on hybrid pricing. Everyone else—ServiceNow, Workday, standalone fintechs, model labs without hyperscaler partnerships—is caught. Winners going forward own either: (1) platform moats (10+ year customer relationships where agents are embedded), or (2) infrastructure moats (compute capacity, payment rails, regulatory licenses). Application-layer companies are dependent on whoever controls (1) or (2).

## The Key Tension
**Where will the repricing stop?** The market is pricing SaaS -30–45% YTD assuming margin compression is *permanent*. This is technically correct—consumption pricing creates visible variable costs that cap gross margins at 45–55% vs. historical 80%+. But Salesforce ($800M Agentforce ARR) and HubSpot (Q1 margin targets hit early) are proving execution speed on pricing mechanics matters more than scale. Meanwhile, Google's refusal to give Meta compute capacity in March 2026 reveals that infrastructure bottlenecks—not model capability—are now the limiting factor. If hyperscalers can guarantee capacity for labs that hit $1B+ ARR (Amazon-Anthropic model), then independent labs stay independent but lose margin. If not, consolidation accelerates. The repricing assumes hyperscalers will consolidate *all* labs that threaten their margin. Evidence suggests hyperscalers actually prefer a mixed ecosystem (some independent labs paying premium prices for capacity, some internal teams competing on margin) to ensure bargaining power.
