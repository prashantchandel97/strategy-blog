## Core Thesis
Per-seat SaaS pricing is collapsing because AI agents eliminate the need for human seats—but the "solution" (consumption-based pricing) replaces zero-variable-cost software delivery with high-variable-cost compute delivery, shifting profit margins away from SaaS vendors to cloud and model providers. Only consolidators ($1B+ ARR, 10+ year customer lock-in) can absorb this margin compression and leverage AI as a bundled moat; everyone else is trapped in structurally lower margins. Salesforce's Agentforce ($800M ARR, 169% YoY growth) is the only large-scale proof the new model works, but even it relies on platform dominance and customer switching costs, not unit economics innovation.

## The Key Tension
**The repricing looks severe but may be correct:** SaaS stocks down 30–45% YTD suggests the market is repricing for permanent margin compression, which is rational. Yet Salesforce and HubSpot are hitting growth and margin targets simultaneously, implying consolidators *can* navigate the transition. The tension: is the -40% repricing too harsh on the broad SaaS market (because consolidators will re-rate as consumption stabilizes), or is it accurately separating long-term winners from commoditized also-rans? The answer determines whether SaaS rebounds in 18–24 months or remains structurally cheaper forever.

## Key Facts & Data Points
- Agentforce (Salesforce's agentic AI platform) reached $800M ARR in Q4 FY26, growing 169% YoY with 29,000 agent deals closed — signals consolidators can monetize AI without losing growth velocity. (investor.salesforce.com, Salesforce Q4 FY26 earnings)
- 37% of SaaS companies now use hybrid pricing models (seat + consumption), up from ~25% in 2025; Gartner forecasts 40% will use outcome-based pricing by end of 2026, up from 15% in 2022 — rapid adoption but still in transition. (Kyle Poyar's 2026 State of B2B Monetization survey; Gartner, softwareseni.com)
- Seat-based revenue share expected to fall from 21% of SaaS spend in 2024 to 15% by 2026 (30% decline in 24 months) — existential shift in unit economics. (Gartner)
- Consumption-based pricing creates direct variable costs: every LLM token Salesforce or Freshworks serves costs real compute dollars from OpenAI, Anthropic, or AWS, capping gross margins at ~45–55% vs. historical 80%+ on seat-based delivery. (Janelle Teng Wade analysis, nextbigteng.substack.com)
- HubSpot hit margin targets a year early in Q1 2026 while navigating pricing reset and raised guidance — consolidator advantage in absorbing transition friction evident. (HubSpot Q1 2026 earnings, Investing.com, TIKR)
- Freshworks' "sessions" consumption metric is growing faster than traditional seat growth, but pricing (e.g., $100 per 1,000 sessions) embeds inference costs to OpenAI/Anthropic — proof of concept, but structurally lower margin. (CEO Dennis Woodside, CNBC TV18, May 2026)
- Salesforce down 30–40% YTD; Adobe down 45%; ServiceNow down 40%; Workday down 26–38%; Monday.com down 25% — valuations compressed across board, but large consolidators outperforming smaller pure-plays. (TIKR, 24/7 Wall St, FinancialContent)
- 43% of enterprise buyers express demand for outcome-based pricing (e.g., pricing on "faster deal cycles" rather than seats), but vendors rarely deliver at scale due to complexity in isolating agent contribution to business outcome. (Gartner)
- Profit pool is migrating from SaaS vendors to cloud and model infrastructure providers: Azure, AWS, OpenAI, and Anthropic now capture the margin layer between customer and SaaS product. (Nvidia executive commentary, Fortune, May 2026)
