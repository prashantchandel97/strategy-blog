## Core Thesis
We're in a *disaggregation moment*: capability is becoming commodity, integration/compliance are becoming the moat. Frontier models (GPT-5.2, Gemini 3.1, Claude Opus) now outperform specialized AI on benchmarks—but regulatory/data/governance moats give specialized companies 3–5 years breathing room. SaaS per-seat pricing broke because agents don't need seats; execution speed on repricing (Salesforce, HubSpot winning) determines winners in 12–18 month transition. Anthropic hit $1B+ ARR and partners with Amazon to stay independent; Mistral couldn't and got acquired at 40x revenue. Baseten abstracts compute pricing across clouds and grows at 1,900% YoY—but that moat lasts 2–3 years before hyperscalers compete. The unifying pattern: companies owning capability alone (vertical AI startups, slow SaaS vendors, independent labs) are losing. Companies owning *integration/compliance/governance* (Harvey pivoting to agent orchestration, Salesforce locking outcome pricing, Epic/Cerner data moats) are repositioning to survive. The bundle is breaking. Value is migrating from "owning capability" to "controlling compliance, data, or distribution."

## The Key Tension
**When does the disaggregation stabilize?** Frontier models have already won the capability race (Nature Medicine: GPT-5.2 beats specialized medical AI by 7–18 percentage points). But specialized vendors with regulatory moats (FDA clearance), data moats (EHR integration), or governance moats (explainability) can survive 3–5 years before those moats erode. Meanwhile, SaaS consolidators executing fast on repricing (HubSpot hitting margin targets early) are re-rating upward—suggesting the -40% repricing is too harsh because execution is faster than the market priced. Baseten's 1,900% YoY growth on abstraction layers proves enterprises *do* escape lock-in when given the option—but hyperscalers (AWS, Azure, GCP) will kill that moat in 2027–2028 by building competing products. So valuations across all three categories (SaaS down 40%, specialized AI 3–5 year reprieve, abstraction layers at 21x revenue) are internally inconsistent. They can't all be right. The market is pricing for different expiration dates without acknowledging it.

## Open Questions for the Blog
1. **If frontier models beat specialized AI on capability, why do regulatory/data/governance moats extend reprieve 3–5 years instead of collapsing immediately?** Harvey pivoting to agent orchestration buys time, but does FDA clearance actually prevent GPT-5 from entering regulated healthcare if OpenAI wanted to? Or is it liability/organizational inertia, not regulation? Timeline matters for whether specialized vendors can actually pivot or just delay death.
2. **Is the -40% SaaS repricing correct, or too harsh given execution speed?** HubSpot and Salesforce moving fast on repricing and showing margin recovery. Does this mean -40% SaaS selloff gets compressed to -20% by Q4 2026 once market sees execution speed? Or is the repricing right because consumption pricing *structurally* caps margins forever, regardless of speed?
3. **Who actually wins the bundle disaggregation—infrastructure providers or application vendors?** Frontier models own capability (commodity). Hyperscalers own infrastructure (margin moat). But application vendors (Harvey, Salesforce, Epic) own integration/compliance/workflow. In 2028, who captures the most margin: the hyperscaler providing compute, or the app vendor selling workflow? This determines whether consolidators win despite margin compression.
