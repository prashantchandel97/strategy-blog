## Core Thesis
AI agents have shattered the 25-year SaaS unit economics model by eliminating the need for human seats—and the migration to consumption-based pricing is not a clever pivot, but a forced surrender of margin to cloud and model infrastructure providers. However, the real winners aren't broad "consolidators," but rather companies that can simultaneously (1) embed agents into existing customer relationships, (2) own pricing mechanics (be first-mover on outcome-based or hybrid models), and (3) control a high-switching-cost layer (regulatory, data, implementation, or customer lock-in). Salesforce ($800M Agentforce ARR, 169% YoY growth) and HubSpot (Q1 2026 margin targets hit a year early) are executing this triple play; ServiceNow, Workday, and most of SaaS are not. The consolidation narrative is real, but execution speed on pricing matters more than existing scale.

## The Key Tension
**Is the SaaS repricing (-30–45% YTD) structural or cyclical?** The market is pricing for permanent margin compression, which is technically correct—consumption-based pricing does create variable costs that cap margins at ~45–55% vs. historical 80%+. But consolidators with platform moats (Salesforce, HubSpot) are proving they can absorb the compression *while maintaining growth velocity* if they move fast on pricing mechanics. The tension: slow-movers (ServiceNow, Workday) might be repriced permanently because execution is lagging, while fast-movers (HubSpot) could re-rate upward once consumption pricing stabilizes. The outcome depends on whether the market is separating winners from losers (execution-based) or just permanently discounting all SaaS (sector-wide). Early evidence suggests the former—but the repricing assumes the latter.
