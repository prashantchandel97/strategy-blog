## Core Thesis
Two seemingly unrelated markets—India fintech and AI infrastructure—are consolidating via the same pattern: companies moving early to capture arbitrage on regulatory or market separation, then defending through compliance/optimization moats that will compress once the constraint opens. Kissht captured lending before RBI opens to tech giants; Google is extracting inference margins before token commoditization kills the advantage. This is not a story about fintech or chips. It's a story about *timing*: which consolidation plays happened early enough to build defensible barriers before arbitrage windows close?

## The Key Tension
The Researcher identifies real moats (measurement in lending, custom silicon in inference) and treats them as defensible long-term advantages. The Analyst flips the frame: these are *arbitrages on temporary separation*, and their defensibility depends entirely on *when* the constraint opens (RBI opens lending to tech giants; token commodities emerge). If separation closes in 12-18 months, even profitable incumbent advantages evaporate. The strongest unresolved question: do these arbitrage windows stay open long enough (2028+) for Kissht, Google, and other early movers to consolidate and become too entrenched to dislodge, or do they close fast enough (2027) that incumbents get crushed? The answer determines whether this week's consolidation plays are prescient bets or perfectly-timed exits ahead of margin compression.

Can a well-capitalized late mover (Google entering lending, AMD entering AI chips) replicate moats faster than incumbent can?
3. **Consolidation playbook**: What's the pattern that separates "perfectly timed consolidation that becomes defensible" (Kissht, Google) from "consolidation that gets disrupted when constraint opens" (potential fate of Razorpay if it enters lending too late)? Can investors identify which plays are timed right?
