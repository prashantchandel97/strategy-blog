## Core Thesis
Consolidation across fintech, AI infrastructure, and enterprise SaaS is not a game of timing—move early, extract pricing, build next moat—but a game of **validation scope**. The companies that win are those whose moats have been validated in conditions representative of where they'll deploy. Zillow lost $880M validating on past prices, deploying to future prediction. Kissht (2.12% GNPA, industry-average but untested against tech giants) is borderline. Google (44% TCO advantage proven internally, unproven on external heterogeneous customer workloads) has fallback. Salesforce (56% customer ROI failure = zero validation) is very high risk. Historical parallels prove the pattern: Intel refused to revalidate when foundry economics changed (moat became 10-year anchor, 2015-2025). Microsoft revalidated, saw cloud was superior, pivoted successfully (85% cloud revenue by 2026). Cisco extrapolated without intermediate proof (Cerent $6.9B acquisition never recovered). **The four-test validation framework (validation context, moat strength, commoditization timeline, next-moat defensibility) scores Google 7.25/10 (likely winner), Kissht 5.25/10 (borderline, RBI timing critical), Salesforce 2.75/10 (very high risk unless Q1 2027 earnings show dramatic ROI improvement). Consolidation speed doesn't predict outcomes. Validation scope does.**
