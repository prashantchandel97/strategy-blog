## Core Thesis
The SaaS pricing crisis is not future-facing: the market repriced it in January 2026 when agentic AI became reliable. What's happening now is a fragmented transition where enterprises (Salesforce, Workday customers) stay loyal but shift to consumption models over 3–5 years, while the mid-market (smaller, generic workflows) bleeds to open-source agents and cheaper startups. Gartner's $234 billion "at risk" number is real, but it describes margin erosion, not cliff collapse. The competitive winner will be vendors that simplify billing, execute agent speed, and leverage hyperscaler partnerships—not just those fastest to abandon per-seat.

## The Key Tension
The Analyst confirms the Researcher's structural insight (per-seat pricing breaks under agentic AI) but challenges the timeline and victim pool. The Researcher implies a 18-month decisive crisis where vendors must choose consumption or die; the Analyst argues enterprises have deeper integration locks (3–5 year stickiness) and will pay for controlled migration. The real erosion happens in the mid-market via open-source and vertical agents—a slow leak, not a rupture. This raises a uncomfortable question: are SaaS stocks already fairly valued after the Jan-Apr 2026 crash, or is there another 20–30% compression coming as consumption models compress margins in mature verticals (CRM, HR, support)?

## Open Questions for the Blog
1. **Which SaaS vertical defects first—and is it the vendor shifting or the customer switching?** If a Fortune 500 announces a major Salesforce/Workday customer defection to open-source agents in Q4 2026, the narrative shifts from "pricing model evolution" to "product obsolescence." That's the inflection point to watch.
2. **Will consumption pricing actually compress SaaS margins, or is the real hit to growth trajectory?** If Salesforce's consumption model customers pay 70–80% of per-seat costs, the company doesn't collapse—it just grows slower. Is the blog story "SaaS margins compressed by agentic AI" or "SaaS growth stalls"? That's a very different investor conversation.
3. **Who wins the hyperscaler distribution fight—SaaS vendors or the cloud providers themselves?** If Microsoft and Amazon can sell agents cheaper via their platforms than Salesforce can via Agentforce, the game is over. Is the blog ultimately about SaaS disruption, or about cloud consolidation?
