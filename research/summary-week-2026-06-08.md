## Core Thesis
The per-seat SaaS pricing model is broken. Atlassian's revenue grew 21% while seat count fell in Q1 2026—the link between "people using software" and "software value delivered" has snapped. But the crisis isn't apocalyptic. The winners are companies that measure customer outcomes precisely enough to command premium pricing for 3-7 years before the moat erodes. **Measurement moats outlast infrastructure moats (which last 12-18 months) by 3-5x.** Example: Eli Lilly captured 51% of the GLP-1 market against Novo Nordisk's 70% price cut, proven moat: superior weight loss efficacy data (22.5% vs. 15-17%). Slack will commoditize (productivity unmeasurable). Zendesk will thrive until 2029-2030 (resolution quality measurable). The real battlefield: SaaS companies in measurable categories survive; unmeasurable ones die.

## The Key Tension
**The hierarchy of moat durability:** Infrastructure moats (12-18 months) < Measurement moats (3-7 years) < Distribution advantage (indefinite). But distribution has a fatal flaw: it doesn't guarantee measurement credibility. OpenAI has 3M Codex users vs. Cognition's 80K Devin users. Should have crushed Devin. Didn't—because Devin's switching costs (process embedding + institutional measurement) are stickier than routing advantages. **But** OpenAI builds competing measurement data 40x faster. Cognition's moat compresses from "3-7 years defensible" to "18-24 months before OpenAI's measurement catches up." The tension: measurement is the strongest moat **until** a competitor with 40x distribution arrives. Then distribution beats measurement. The question for the blog: what comes after measurement? The evidence points to contractual outcome guarantees (vendor liable if customer doesn't hit promised result). ServiceNow is betting on this. Is it actually defensible, or just the next temporary moat?
