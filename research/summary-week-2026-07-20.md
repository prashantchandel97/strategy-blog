## Core Thesis
Three infrastructure markets are fracturing along a single axis: **vendor lock-in is weaker than it appears**. Anthropic's 33,000-token system preamble burns 4.7x more tokens than open alternatives, but only matters if OpenCode can match production quality. Nvidia's July 1 revenue-share financing model theoretically locks in neocloud providers, but actually transfers supplier risk while customers retain refinancing options (CoreWeave is already raising venture capital alongside Nvidia deals). Salesforce is defending SaaS by bundling AI and moving to outcome-based pricing—but the $234B "at risk" is mostly in low-moat SMB categories, not enterprise core where data lock-in protects incumbents. The disruption is real; the defensibility is stronger than Monday's headlines suggested.

## The Key Tension
**Lock-In vs. Customer Leverage**: Monday's researcher argued that Nvidia's revenue-share model "strengthens lock-in" because refinancing requires balance-sheet restructuring. Tuesday's analyst found the counter-evidence: AMD's MI355X now outperforms Nvidia on inference (30% faster, 40% better tokens-per-dollar), cloud providers are running GPU-agnostic containerized workloads, and Sequoia + Accel are actively financing alternatives without Nvidia revenue-share lock-in. The real question is not whether Nvidia's financing is a moat—it's whether Nvidia's *concentration risk* (61% revenue from 4 customers) is an existential threat that revenue-share doesn't solve. If hyperscalers continue accelerating custom silicon (Google TPUs, Amazon Trainium, Meta custom accelerators), Nvidia's financing model becomes a liability: creditor risk on depreciating collateral.
