## Core Thesis
AI is exposing the fundamental fragility of vendor lock-in models built around consumption metrics (tokens, users, transactions). Anthropic burns 4.7x more tokens than open alternatives, but this only matters if the alternative achieves production parity. Nvidia's revenue-share financing unlocks neocloud growth but doesn't prevent customer refinancing or GPU-agnostic deployments—the real threat is hyperscaler custom silicon. Salesforce and enterprise SaaS defend through data lock-in and outcome-based bundling, not per-user pricing. The disruptors are those that shift from monetizing consumption to defensibility: data, regulatory access, and ecosystems. Razorpay's first-mover advantage in agentic payments is structural in India (PA-CB license) but ephemeral globally—the 24-month window closes when incumbents move.

## The Key Tension
**Consumption Models Are Breaking, But Winners Are Already Repositioning**

The Researcher correctly identifies three separate consumption-based models fracturing under AI pressure: tokens (Anthropic), GPUs (Nvidia), and per-seat SaaS (Salesforce). The Analyst adds the critical counter-narrative: disruption is not evenly distributed. In SMB/low-moat categories, it's real and fast. In enterprise core (CRM, ERP, HCM), it's overstated because switching costs are data-embedded, not feature-based. The strongest tension: if Salesforce moves fast to outcome-based pricing and embeds agents into licenses, it survives. If it doesn't, its market cap takes a 40-50% haircut by 2028. Nvidia faces similar pressure but cannot move as fast—custom silicon by hyperscalers is an existential risk Nvidia-financed neoclouds don't solve.

Razorpay adds another angle: geographic defensibility (regulatory access) can create a moat in the near term (24 months), but global incumbents move faster than expected once they prioritize. Stripe's Sessions 2026 announcements show agentic commerce is already a priority, not a roadmap item.
