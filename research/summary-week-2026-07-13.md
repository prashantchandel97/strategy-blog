## Core Thesis
Meta is making three simultaneous capital bets—AI infrastructure, custom silicon, and fintech distribution—but they are competing for rationed capex, not reinforcing each other. Meta's free cash flow turns negative in 2026 and reaches -$24B in 2027, forcing hard prioritization by H2 2026. AI infrastructure ($110B+) and custom silicon ($15-20B) are non-negotiable defensive bets. Fintech distribution ($0-5B allocated, $4-6B needed) is discretionary and gets deferred to a 5-7 year play. This signals Meta is optimizing for competitive survival (maintain ad-ranking position against Google, don't lose to AWS/Azure) rather than market expansion (win new fintech revenue). The capital constraint reveals the real strategy: infrastructure and silicon are necessary; fintech is optionality.

## The Key Tension
Meta's three bets face distinct execution risks that the capital constraint exacerbates. AI infrastructure: Zuckerberg admitted (July 2) that agent development is slower than expected, undermining the overcapacity arbitrage thesis; selling cheap compute to competitors (OpenAI, Anthropic) accelerates commoditization of cloud margins. Custom silicon: locked into TSMC through 2027-2028; TSMC's recent CoWoS capacity expansion (120-140K units by end 2026) suggests the bottleneck was temporary, not structural, making Meta's custom-silicon hedge potentially over-invested. Fintech: PhonePe's 46% UPI dominance and upcoming $9.5B IPO narrow the window for WhatsApp's 0.65% payment share; India's antitrust ruling forbids WhatsApp-Meta data-sharing, destroying the cross-leverage that justified the $900M CRED investment; Kunal Shah hit friction with WhatsApp leadership within 17 days of appointment, signaling underfunding. The deeper problem: fintech deferred for 5-7 years means Meta cedes the emerging-market fintech market to PhonePe and foregoes transformational growth to defend infrastructure margins that may prove temporary.

The question is whether PhonePe's moat (proven unit economics, merchant network, lending products) becomes so entrenched that Meta's late entry (2030+) faces structural disadvantage. 3. **Is TSMC's capacity constraint structural or temporary?** TSMC's recent CoWoS expansion (120-140K units by end 2026) contradicts the bottleneck narrative. If the constraint was temporary, Meta's custom-silicon investment may be defensive hedge against non-existent threat, not strategic necessity. This hinges on TSMC's Q3/Q4 2026 capacity guidance—if TSMC signals CoWoS is reaching equilibrium, custom silicon becomes less urgent and fintech funding becomes more defensible.
