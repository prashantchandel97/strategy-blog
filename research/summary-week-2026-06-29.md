## Core Thesis
Outcome-based pricing and profitable margins are now table stakes, but they don't guarantee survival. The structural winners of 2026 are those who chose regulatory defensibility over feature innovation (Salesforce's Flex Credits, Razorpay's PA licenses). But this is a **legal defense, not a true business moat.** Regulatory enforcement moved faster than business model adaptation (FTC algorithmic pricing 2026 priority, EU AI Act Aug 2, RBI payment aggregator rules forcing ₹25 crore net worth), squeezing Accenture ($240M contract cuts), Turtlemint (11% IPO discount despite profitability), and INFLUISH (regulatory barriers forcing acquihire). The survivors bought time for 5-10 years, but they still face margin compression if regulatory priorities shift (2027-2028 enforcement on monopoly rents or margin extraction) or if scaled competitors get their own regulatory licenses (PhonePe/Paytm PA licenses would collapse Razorpay's moat). Result: The market is selecting for regulatory defensibility as table stakes, but has not yet identified who will win underneath.

## The Key Tension
The week revealed a paradox: **regulatory defensibility looks like a moat but is actually fragile.** Salesforce and Razorpay survived by solving for regulatory risk as a constraint, which is correct and necessary. But consumption-based pricing only defends against *current* FTC scrutiny (deception, discrimination). If the FTC shifts enforcement to "are consumption models forcing larger commitments than needed?" (likely 2027-2028), Salesforce's defensibility flips to liability. Similarly, Razorpay's PA license barrier keeps out startups but not competitors with capital (PhonePe, Paytm, Google Pay seeking their own licenses). Regulatory moats are time-bound. The real question is whether these companies build underlying business moats (lock-in, switching costs, margin advantage) fast enough to survive the next regulatory shift. Evidence is mixed.

## Open Questions for the Blog
1. **Are regulatory moats actually durable, or just table stakes?** Salesforce and Razorpay won 2026 by solving regulatory risk. But if FTC enforcement shifts in 2027-2028 (from "deception/discrimination" to "margin extraction"), today's defensibility becomes liability. Same for Razorpay if PhonePe/Paytm get PA licenses and the fee competition intensifies. Do the 2026 winners remain winners in 2028-2029, or have they just bought 5-10 years of time?

2. **Which business models can survive regulatory *and* architectural shifts simultaneously?** Accenture tried outcome-based + AI displacement + FTC scrutiny = failed. Salesforce chose consumption + traditional licensing + CRM lock-in = survived. Is the pattern that only companies with massive existing lock-in (Salesforce, Razorpay with capital barriers) can absorb regulatory compliance costs, or can smaller companies also survive by picking the right technical + regulatory path early?

3. **When does the next regulatory shift happen, and what will it target?** FTC 2026 = algorithmic pricing deception. RBI 2025-2026 = payment aggregator capital requirements. What's next: margin extraction, monopoly rents, or something else? If the next target is consumption models themselves (are they forcing larger commitments than needed?), then Salesforce's defensibility flips to liability within 24 months.
