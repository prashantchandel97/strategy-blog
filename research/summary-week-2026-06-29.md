## Core Thesis
The market is compressing via three simultaneous shifts: (1) **pricing inversion** — outcome-based models hit a regulatory ceiling at 20-30% adoption, losing to consumption-based pricing that passes FTC scrutiny; (2) **architectural disruption** — agent networks and feature-based moats fail against fundamentally different value chains (UPI embedded distribution beats insurance agents; AI agents replace consulting headcount); (3) **regulatory moat fragility** — companies that solved 2026's regulatory risk (Salesforce Flex Credits, Razorpay PA licenses) bought 5-10 years of defensibility but now face 2027-2028 antitrust enforcement targeting market concentration and exclusionary conduct. Result: Accenture ($240M contract cuts), Turtlemint (11% IPO discount despite +12% EBITDA), INFLUISH (₹25 crore regulatory barrier forcing acquihire) all failed because they were hit by two shifts simultaneously. Salesforce and Razorpay survived by choosing one regulatory path, but remain vulnerable to the next shift.

## The Key Tension
**Regulatory defensibility is necessary but not durable.** Companies solved 2026 FTC enforcement (algorithmic pricing deception) by moving to transparent consumption models (Salesforce) or regulatory licenses (Razorpay). But this creates exposure to 2027-2028 antitrust enforcement: Razorpay's 30-40% payment market share becomes monopoly risk if CCI finds exclusionary conduct. Salesforce's CRM dominance + Agentforce bundling becomes market-leveraging risk if courts apply Microsoft IE bundling precedent. The regulatory cycle is: Cycle 1 (2024-2026) = deception enforcement → consumption-based defensibility. Cycle 2 (2027-2028) = antitrust enforcement on concentration → defensibility flips to liability. Companies preparing only for Cycle 1 while ignoring Cycle 2 preparation will face structural remedies (divestitures, unbundling, forced licensing) by 2028-2029.
