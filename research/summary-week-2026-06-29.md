## Core Thesis
Outcome-based pricing is not replacing seat-based SaaS — vendors are hiding in hybrid models (base seats + consumption/outcome fees) to avoid the attribution trap. Accenture's June 18 guidance cut (-18%, growth down to 3-4%) signals that AI displacement has made traditional per-seat pricing indefensible, but pure outcome-based pricing creates recurring measurement disputes that kill profitability. The market will converge on hybrid models (already at 60%+ adoption) because they preserve vendor ambiguity about who caused the outcome. This suggests a prolonged, contested transition where the only real moat is unambiguous measurement — a capability most vendors lack and actively avoid investing in.

## The Key Tension
The Researcher argues outcome-based pricing is inevitable because AI agents have made human headcount obsolete. The Analyst counters that vendors will fight *outcome-based adoption* because it creates attribution disputes that are harder to defend than seat-based pricing — so they're choosing hybrid models instead, which gives them the illusion of adapting while maintaining vendor optionality and avoiding transparent measurement. This means outcome adoption will hit a ceiling (20-30%), not inflect exponentially, and most of the value will stay locked in hybrid arrangements where vendors can argue "both the vendor and client contributed to the outcome."

## Open Questions for the Blog
1. **Which outcome-based use cases have the lowest attribution risk?** Fintech fraud prevention (fraud rate is unambiguous) vs. consulting time savings (attribution is contested). Does the low-risk category cap outcome-based adoption at 15-20% of total SaaS revenue?
2. **How long can hybrid models survive before customers demand pure outcome pricing?** ServiceNow is sticky, but will mid-market SaaS vendors be forced to choose between losing margin (pure outcome) or losing customers (staying hybrid)?
3. **Will vendors ever invest in third-party measurement to solve attribution?** (E.g., Salesforce forces all Agentforce metrics through independent audit.) Or does vendor optionality matter too much — they'd rather keep the ambiguity that lets them argue for value?
