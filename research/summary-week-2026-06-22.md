## Core Thesis
Enterprise AI's 95% pilot failure rate has two concurrent solutions: measurement discipline (identifying problems where ROI is measurable before scaling) and custom silicon cost collapse (Amazon Trainium $20B+, Google TPUs $25B by 2027, Microsoft Maia 200). Together, they solve the economic problem from both angles. But TSMC's 3nm capacity is fully utilized with demand 3x supply, meaning hyperscalers can't scale custom chips fast enough to hit 40-50% inference displacement before 2028-2029. **The real dividing line in 2027-28 will be: Who has access to low-cost compute infrastructure (cloud providers, insiders) vs. who pays market rates?** Measurement discipline only matters if your cost structure is competitive.

## The Key Tension
Monday framed the problem as "measurement discipline separates winners from losers." But Tuesday's custom silicon data suggests the causality is reversed: **measurement discipline diagnoses unfavorable unit economics, but it doesn't fix them.** A company with perfect measurement discipline and Nvidia-GPU pricing discovers that AI ROI is negative. The same company with mediocre discipline and custom-chip pricing discovers ROI is positive. The real winners may not be more disciplined; they may be more *integrated*—i.e., customers of AWS/Google Cloud who get custom-silicon cost structures automatically. Conversely, TSMC capacity constraints mean hyperscalers can't ship custom silicon fast enough to make this true for everyone by 2027, giving Nvidia an accidental reprieve.
