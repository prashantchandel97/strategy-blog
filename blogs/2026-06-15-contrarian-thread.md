1/ Here is the strongest case against everything I just argued.

2/ Salesforce has 150,000+ customers with multi-year enterprise contracts and massive switching costs (migrating CRM data takes 12-18 months and costs millions). Even if Agentforce ROI is weak, inertia keeps them locked in. Churn might never come.

3/ Intel is a counter-example to my own framework. They scored terribly on validation scope but survived for 10 years bleeding margins. Sometimes a bad moat still extracts billions in cash flow. Collapse speed matters as much as collapse probability.

4/ The validation scope framework assumes companies will revalidate honestly. But executives have career incentives to keep extracting on the current moat even when they know it is failing. Salesforce insiders sell the stock while bundling Agentforce. They know the game.

5/ Both things can be true. Validation scope predicts long-term winners, but short-term extraction can still be wildly profitable. The outcome depends on your time horizon: if you are building for 10 years, revalidate. If you are optimizing for next quarter, bundle and extract.
