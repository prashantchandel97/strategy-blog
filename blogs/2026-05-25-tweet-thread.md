# Tweet Thread: Governance Is the New Moat

1/ Uber burned through their entire 2026 AI budget in 4 months. Engineers shipped faster. But when leadership asked what changed in the actual product or P&L, the answer was zero. Not "underwhelming" — literally nothing measurable.

2/ This is not an AI problem. PwC found 56% of CEOs report zero ROI from AI investments. MIT studied enterprise pilots: 95% deliver zero P&L impact despite working technology. The bottleneck is not model quality — it's that nobody scoped what success meant before they started.

3/ McKinsey found the 5% that succeed all do one thing differently: they measure outcomes before deploying AI. Organizations with measurement discipline get 60%+ ROI. Without it, 29%. The difference is asking "what are we trying to change?" before buying the tool.

4/ The same governance gap is deciding winners in fintech. Razorpay just posted Rs 1,209 crore loss — not from operations, but from regulatory redomiciling. That's 32% of annual revenue, paid to satisfy compliance. Small fintechs cannot absorb that cost. Scale is the moat now.

5/ And in platforms: Arm launched their own CPU, competing with the companies they license to. The FTC opened an antitrust probe within weeks. Microsoft bundling Copilot triggered a UK investigation. Platform owners using control to compete downstream now face automatic scrutiny.

6/ Across AI, fintech, platforms, and SaaS, the pattern is identical: technology became infrastructure, so the moat shifted from building better tools to deploying them with discipline. Governance constraints now decide winners — the question is whether that's learnable or structural.
