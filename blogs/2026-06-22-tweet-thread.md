# Tweet Thread: Data Structure Beats Discipline

1/ Stripe trades at 57x revenue. Salesforce at 8.5x. HubSpot at 7.2x. Zendesk at 2.1x. Wall Street thinks the gap is execution quality. The data says it's something else: fintech work is inherently data-structured, and sales work isn't.

2/ 95% of enterprise AI pilots fail to produce measurable ROI. But fintech succeeds: Stripe's fraud detection cuts fraud 32%, Razorpay by 40%. Why? Transactions carry 300+ structured signals, fraud is binary (yes or no), and measurement is automatic.

3/ Finance operations deploying AI hit 8-month ROI payback. Sales and marketing AI? 18-24+ months, if ever. This isn't a discipline gap. You can't measure what you can't structure into data, and relationship-driven work resists clean measurement.

4/ Amazon, Google, and Microsoft custom chips cut AI costs 40-50%. But this only helps if ROI was already plausible. It makes fintech margins better. It doesn't fix broken sales AI attribution. Cost reduction accelerates bifurcation, it doesn't solve it.

5/ Fintech SaaS trades at a 1.5x premium over horizontal SaaS today (10-15x revenue vs. 4-8x). But ROI differences (8-month vs. 18-24 month payback) justify 3x. The repricing is coming. Fintech should hit 20-25x while horizontal compresses to 5-7x by 2028.

6/ Enterprise AI won't accelerate broadly. It will deepen sharply in data-native domains (fintech, finance, operations) and stall everywhere else. The companies that win won't be the ones with the best discipline. They'll be the ones where work is already data-native.
