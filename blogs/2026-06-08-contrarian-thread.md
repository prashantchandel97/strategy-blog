1/ Here is the strongest case against everything I just argued.

2/ Measurement moats assume customers are rational. They are not. Slack has zero measurement proof but 70% of teams stick with it at 125% higher prices because switching costs (retraining, integration rebuilds) exceed rational ROI calculations.

3/ Eli Lilly did not win on efficacy data alone. They won because doctors prescribe branded drugs with insurance coverage, not generics. The measurement story is clean but the real moat is regulatory capture and distribution through existing pharma sales channels.

4/ Infrastructure moats can last decades if they control standards. Intel held x86 dominance for 20 years not because their chips were better (AMD often matched or beat them) but because software was compiled for x86. Rambus lost because they never controlled the standard.

5/ Both things can be true. The outcome depends on whether measurement translates to switching behavior or just justifies decisions customers already made for other reasons (lock-in, brand, distribution).
