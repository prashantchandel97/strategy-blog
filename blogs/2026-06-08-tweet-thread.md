# Tweet Thread: The Measurement Moat

1/ Atlassian grew revenue 21% while seat count fell for the first time ever. The number of people paying for Jira dropped, but the company made more money. The link between users and value just broke.

2/ Meanwhile Eli Lilly beat a competitor with a 5-year head start and 70% lower pricing by proving one number: 22.5% weight loss vs 15-17%. Measurement beat brand, timing, and price.

3/ Every competitive advantage is temporary. But they break on different schedules. Infrastructure moats (chip designs, APIs) last 12-18 months. Measurement moats last 3-7 years.

4/ Rambus tripled operating income selling memory chips at 80% margins. Then Marvell shipped a 70% more efficient design. Hyperscalers are switching now. The moat evaporates in 15 months because better tech wins fast.

5/ But Lilly's measurement advantage holds until 2028-2030. Why? Proving your drug works better requires years of clinical data, regulatory approval, and real-world validation. Competitors can't copy that in 18 months.

6/ The SaaS market is splitting: companies in measurable categories (customer support, credit underwriting) survive 3-7 years. Unmeasurable categories (team collaboration, productivity tools) commoditize in 18-24 months.

7/ Slack raised prices 125% calling it the AI tax. But they can't measure whether teams are actually more productive. Without proof, premium pricing collapses. Zendesk charges per verified resolution and can prove it works.
