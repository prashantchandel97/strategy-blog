# Tweet Thread: Moat Compression in the AI Era

1/ Razorpay shipped the first agentic payment integration on Feb 11, 2026. They were the only option for AI agents that needed to charge customers. That first-mover advantage lasted exactly 22 days. Stripe matched them on July 9. This is what moat compression looks like.

2/ Claude Code burns 33,000 tokens on tasks OpenCode does in 7,000. That is a 4.7x inefficiency tax. The moat is customer ignorance: most devs do not track cost per task. When someone builds a side-by-side comparison tool, developers switch in weeks. Timer: 12-18 months.

3/ Nvidia launched revenue-share GPU financing in July 2026. Looks like lock-in, but the real threat is not customer switching. It is hyperscalers building custom chips. Meta is spending $145B on MTIA, their custom silicon that runs inference 40% cheaper than Nvidia GPUs.

4/ Gartner says $234 billion in SaaS spending is at risk from AI agents. But the risk is not evenly distributed. SMB customers churn in 12-18 months (low data lock-in, easy migration). Enterprise stays protected for 3-5 years (8+ years of CRM data, integrations, compliance).

5/ Meta is spending $125-145 billion to run AI compute 20-30% below AWS prices. But they are missing the moat AWS spent a decade building: enterprise SLAs. Uptime guarantees, compliance certs, 24/7 support. Meta can hire AWS alumni, but trust takes years. Timeline: 2028-2029.

6/ The pattern is identical across every category. Token overhead gives you 18 months. Distribution defaults last 18-24 months. Regulatory moats survive 3-5 years. The only defensibility that outlasts 24 months is structural: custody, compliance, data lock-in, operational trust.
