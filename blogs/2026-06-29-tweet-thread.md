# Tweet Thread: Regulatory Moats Become Liabilities

1/ Accenture lost $240M in contracts in June 2026, but not to competitors. The culprit: outcome-based pricing (charging for time saved, not hours worked) requires proving causality that no vendor can defend under FTC algorithmic pricing enforcement. Clients walked.

2/ Salesforce survived the same regulatory cycle with zero cancellations. The difference: Flex Credits charge $0.10 per auditable action (email sent, case resolved) instead of unmeasurable outcomes. You can count actions. You cannot audit counterfactuals.

3/ Here is the pattern: regulatory moats last 5-10 years, then enforcement priorities shift. 2024-2026 targeted deception and transparency (FTC pricing rules). 2027-2028 targets market concentration (Google Chrome divestiture, Meta and Uber antitrust verdicts, all June 2026).

4/ Razorpay has a real moat: India's Payment Aggregator license requires 25 crore rupees ($3M) in net worth. That blocks startups. But Razorpay now processes 30-40% of India's digital payments, which becomes monopoly risk under antitrust enforcement starting 2027.

5/ Companies fail when hit by two shifts simultaneously. Accenture: regulatory (FTC) + architectural (AI agents replacing consulting). Turtlemint: regulatory (embedded insurance rules) + architectural (PhonePe distribution bypassing agent networks, earning 35-45% vs 11%).

6/ Surviving companies layer defenses: solve the current regulatory cycle, prepare for the next one, and build product lock-in that survives both. Salesforce has CRM data moats under Flex Credits compliance. But Agentforce bundling creates the exact antitrust risk Microsoft faced.
