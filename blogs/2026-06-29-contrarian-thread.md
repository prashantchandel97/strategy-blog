1/ Here is the strongest case against everything I just argued.

2/ Accenture's $240M loss is not about regulatory moats failing. It is about consulting margins compressing across the board as clients demand ROI proof. McKinsey and BCG face the same pressure but did not blame FTC enforcement. This is pricing power erosion, not regulatory risk.

3/ Salesforce Flex Credits look like regulatory genius until you realize consumption pricing has always been the software endgame (AWS proved it 15 years ago). Calling it a regulatory moat is post-hoc rationalization. They moved to usage pricing because SaaS margins demanded it.

4/ The 5-10 year regulatory cycle theory assumes enforcers are strategic and consistent. Reality: enforcement is political, reactive, and jurisdiction-specific. Google paid $5B in EU fines and kept bundling. Razorpay's India moat means nothing if they expand to Southeast Asia.

5/ Both things can be true. The outcome depends on whether you have a product customers cannot leave regardless of regulatory environment. Salesforce wins because CRM switching costs are brutal, not because Flex Credits are audit-friendly. The moat is the product, not compliance.
