# Tweet Thread: Why Most Moats Fail

1/ Zillow's algorithm predicted home prices with 1.8% error. They spent years proving it worked. Then they tried using it to buy homes and flip them. The error rate jumped to 7.2%. They lost $880 million and shut it down. Same tech, same market, different context.

2/ This is the validation scope problem: the gap between where a moat is proven and where it gets deployed. Zillow validated on past prices (describing what already happened). They deployed to future prices (predicting what would happen next). The moat evaporated.

3/ Intel did the same thing. For 25 years vertical integration (owning your own chip factories) gave them 50-60% margins. Then TSMC got so good at contract manufacturing that outsourcing beat owning. Intel refused to revalidate. Margins collapsed from 56% to 32.7% over 10 years.

4/ Microsoft revalidated early. In 2014 their moat was bundling Windows and Office on PCs. Nadella saw cloud eating that world. They tested cloud subscriptions hard before pivoting. Today 85% of Microsoft revenue is cloud. They revalidated before the old moat died.

5/ Built a scoring framework. Four tests: was the moat validated in conditions matching deployment? How strong after transfer risk? How fast does it commoditize? Is there a next moat? Scored current consolidations: Google TPU 7.25/10, Kissht 5.25/10, Salesforce 2.75/10.

6/ Salesforce bundled AI agents at $550 per user, a 175% price increase. But 56% of customers report under 5% ROI after 6-18 months. They published zero backward-looking customer ROI data. This is price extraction on an unvalidated moat. Churn cliff risk in Q3-Q4 2027.

7/ Consolidation speed does not predict outcomes. Validation scope does. The companies extracting the most pricing power today (Salesforce) face the highest collapse risk. The companies hedging transfer uncertainty (Google with the $920M per month SpaceX deal) are positioning to win.
