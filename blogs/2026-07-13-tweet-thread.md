# Tweet Thread: When Capex Reveals Strategy

1/ Meta spent $19B in Q1 2026 while generating only $12.4B in free cash flow. By 2027, analysts project the company will burn $24B more than it makes. Despite this, Meta is committing $145B to AI infrastructure. Something does not add up.

2/ The cash crunch forced a choice: fund defensive bets (data centers, custom chips) or offensive bets (turning WhatsApp's 500M Indian users into a fintech platform). Meta chose infrastructure. That choice reveals what the company actually believes about AI's future.

3/ Here is the tell: Kunal Shah (CRED founder, $900M valuation) was named WhatsApp CEO on June 22. By July 9, Indian press reported friction over budget. That is 17 days. You only get that kind of tension when the promised capital does not exist.

4/ Meta deferred India fintech for 5-7 years, ceding the market to PhonePe (46.2% UPI share, planning $10B IPO). WhatsApp has 500M users in India but only 0.65% payment share. Distribution without monetization, abandoned because infrastructure spending comes first.

5/ This is the real signal: Meta is betting that infrastructure ownership matters more than market expansion. That quality differentiation in AI will persist, not converge. That vertical integration beats platform middleware. Stripe is betting the exact opposite.

6/ If models commoditize and infrastructure does not matter, Meta wasted tens of billions. If differentiation persists, Stripe built a metering tool with a 3-5 year shelf life. One of them is building for the wrong future. The market decides by 2028-2029.
