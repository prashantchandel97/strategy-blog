Title: "The Measurement Moat: Why Some SaaS Companies Survive 3-7 Years and Others Die in 18 Months"
Word count: ~3,400 words
Topic: SaaS & Enterprise Software

CORE THESIS:
Every competitive moat is temporary, but they break on different schedules. Infrastructure moats (memory interfaces, routing layers) last 12-18 months. Measurement moats—when companies can prove superior customer outcomes with data—last 3-7 years. The SaaS market bifurcates: companies in measurable categories (support quality, credit risk, drug efficacy) survive with premium pricing; unmeasurable categories (productivity, collaboration) commoditize within 18-24 months.

STRUCTURE:
- Hook: Atlassian's revenue grew 21% while seat count fell; Eli Lilly captured 51% of GLP-1 market despite competitor's 70% price cut
- Arc: Traces five different moats across SaaS, infrastructure, pharma, fintech—all temporary, all breaking on different timelines
- Evidence: Zendesk (outcome-based pricing working), Cognition (switching costs stickier than expected), Rambus (infrastructure moat compressing to 15 months), Eli Lilly (measurement beats price/brand), Cashfree (regulatory structure forces measurement)
- Conclusion: Measurement moats are most durable; next evolution is outcome guarantees (vendor liable if results not delivered)
