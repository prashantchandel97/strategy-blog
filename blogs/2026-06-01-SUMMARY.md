## Title
"The Token Tax: Why AI's Cost Problem Just Got Structural"

## Core Argument
AI infrastructure economics are bifurcating, not consolidating. Memory (HBM/DRAM) now accounts for 64-68% of AI chip costs, enabling custom silicon—but CUDA switching costs create a sharp divide: hyperscalers (Google, Amazon, Meta) will build custom chips and compress their own infrastructure costs, while enterprise customers stay locked into Nvidia at premium prices. The real margin crisis arrives when agentic AI token consumption (projected 24x growth by 2030, with agents burning 3.2x-50x more tokens per task) outpaces per-token cost declines—at that inflection, no amount of software lock-in or vendor pricing power will prevent ROI rejection.

## Key Evidence
- Microsoft cancelled most internal Claude Code licenses after 6-month pilot (Dec 2025–June 2026); cost of compute exceeded employee costs
- Uber burned through entire 2026 AI budget in 4 months at 95% adoption ($500–$2,000 per engineer/month)
- Memory now 64-68% of AI accelerator cost, up from ~33% in 2023
- Google-Blackstone $25B TPU venture targeting 500MW by 2027
- Nvidia maintains 73% data center GPU gross margins and 86% market share via CUDA lock-in
- Goldman Sachs projects 24x token consumption growth by 2030
- Agentic AI tasks consume 3.2x to 50x more tokens than simple queries

## New Frameworks Introduced
1. **The Token Tax** — when token consumption growth outpaces per-token cost declines, creating structural ROI walls regardless of vendor lock-in
2. **Market Bifurcation** — hyperscalers who can afford CUDA switching costs vs. mid-market enterprises who cannot
3. **Cost-Shifting vs. Margin Expansion** — SaaS consumption pricing as tactical hedge against token cost variability, not strategic margin capture
