# Tweet Thread: The Enterprise AI OS War — Everyone's Watching the Wrong Fight

🧵 1/ Oracle launched 22 production AI agents in March. It got almost no coverage. Everyone's watching Salesforce vs. ServiceNow fight for the "enterprise AI OS." They're both playing the wrong game.

2/ The dominant narrative: one of Salesforce or ServiceNow will own the enterprise AI operating system — the way AWS owns cloud. The market is pricing a winner-take-most outcome. That frame is a category error.

3/ The better parallel is the enterprise middleware wars of the 2000s. TIBCO, WebMethods, BEA — all pitched the same story: own the orchestration layer, collect a toll on every transaction. Sound familiar?

4/ That war ended in fragmentation. No single vendor won. And in 2018, Salesforce paid $6.5B to acquire MuleSoft — an admission that you cannot own orchestration from the application layer alone.

5/ Salesforce learned that lesson in 2018. Then immediately repeated the mistake with Agentforce. ServiceNow's "AI Control Tower" position is more coherent — it already sits in the cross-system governance layer.

6/ But ServiceNow has a real risk: its moat comes from managing workflows *about* work. When AI agents automate the work directly, ServiceNow's ticket volume could shrink even as each unit gets more valuable.

7/ OpenAI Codex got the most coverage this week — full Mac computer use, 90+ plugins, persistent memory. The Siebel playbook: lock in end-user habit before IT governance can intercept. It's a real threat with a ceiling.

8/ The ceiling: regulated enterprises won't tolerate unaudited desktop agents for long. Governance gaps create windows, not moats. OpenAI gets a wedge. It doesn't get the enterprise.

9/ Here's the framework: Enterprise AI plays out on two timescales. Near-term (0–24 months): Salesforce and ServiceNow win on distribution, speed, and consumption pricing. ServiceNow hit 55x AI consumption growth in 5 months.

10/ Medium-term (24+ months): every agent ultimately reads from or writes back to a system of record. Finance agents write to ERP. Procurement agents write to ERP. Those systems are Oracle and SAP — not Salesforce.

11/ SAP Joule spans 40+ agents and 2,400 skills. Its Cash Management Agent cuts manual cash positioning by 80% in live deployments. But only 3% of SAP customers are using it in production. Deep moat, poor product velocity.

12/ The most underappreciated question: most large enterprises run SAP for finance AND Salesforce for CRM simultaneously. When an agent crosses that boundary — who governs the write-back? Nobody has a clean answer yet.

13/ My take: this ends like the middleware wars — domain oligopolies, not a single winner. The orchestration tax gets collected. The question is whether it goes to Salesforce, ServiceNow, Oracle, SAP, or a company nobody is watching yet. The database always wins. Eventually.
