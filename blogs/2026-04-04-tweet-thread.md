# Tweet Thread: In AI, the Margin Is the Moat

🧵 1/ Anthropic's CEO told investors: "If revenue is $800B instead of $1T, no force on Earth stops me from going bankrupt." A $500B IPO candidate. One bad year from insolvent. This sentence explains the entire 2026 AI market.

2/ There are two kinds of AI companies right now. Margin Sovereigns: Nvidia, Google, Microsoft. Margin Constrained: Anthropic, OpenAI. The boundary between them is structural, not temporary.

3/ The scoreboard is brutal. Nvidia: 75% gross margins, $216B revenue, net positive. OpenAI: 33% gross margins, $24B ARR, $25B+ projected burn in 2026. One of these is a business. The other is a race against insolvency.

4/ OpenAI tripled revenue in 2025. Also burned $9B doing it. Gross margins fell from 40% to 33% as inference costs hit $8.4B. You can grow fast and still be running in place. That's the treadmill.

5/ The usual counter: AWS ran thin margins for years, then became the most profitable cloud business in history. So maybe Anthropic does the same? It's a good argument. Here's why it breaks.

6/ AWS's margins expanded because software has near-zero marginal cost. More customers, same infrastructure. AI inference is hardware-bound. Every query needs GPU cycles. Every GPU cycle costs money. The math is structurally different.

7/ Google proved this. They cut Gemini serving costs 78% in 2025 — using Ironwood TPUs delivering 4x better performance-per-dollar than Nvidia H100s. That took a decade and tens of billions. Anthropic doesn't have captive silicon. Google does.

8/ Google is the most underrated player in this race. Cloud at 30%+ operating margins on $17.7B/quarter. That operating margin is higher than Anthropic's blended gross margin. Let that land.

9/ Nvidia's move is the most elegant of all. It pulled back from $40B in OpenAI/Anthropic equity — then acquired Groq for $20B. Not equity in customers. Ownership of the inference architecture. It doesn't need to own its customers. Just their only viable infrastructure.

10/ Microsoft is the landlord with a nervous tenant. OpenAI signed a $250B Azure commitment — then signed a $38B AWS deal six days later. Microsoft reportedly considered suing. That's not victory lap behavior.

11/ Three paths for Anthropic and OpenAI. A: Build captive silicon, reach 77% margins, become infrastructure. B: Get acquired by a Tier 1 player. C: IPO in 2026, race the clock. Most are pricing Path A. Evidence points to Path C trending toward B.

12/ The Anthropic S-1 will be the most important IPO disclosure in tech history. One number decides everything: are enterprise contracts margin-positive while API usage is deeply negative? If yes, it's mix-shift math. If no, it's venture capital subsidizing growth.

13/ The AI value stack looks like the smartphone profit pool. Apple had 20-27% unit share and captured 85% of industry profit. Nvidia's GPU share is declining from 87% to 75% — but at 75% gross margins on $216B revenue, every retained point is disproportionate profit. Same model.
